# Whole home

You don't add the whole home yourself: Power Insight creates it from the
devices you have set up. Its sensors live on two devices:

- **Combined**: totals across your whole home. The device name already says
  "combined", so the sensor names don't repeat it.
- **Home base load** (optional): everything your home uses that has **no
  sensor of its own**, meaning total consumption minus your consumers.

Turn the sensors on in the integration's **Options → Combined sensors**.

All power entering your home (grid import, solar and battery discharge),
split by [where it goes](../sensors.mdx#where-power-goes):

- **Home consumption** is everything your appliances draw, metered or not,
  from any source.
- **Battery charging** is power going into your batteries.
- **System standby** is the idle draw of your energy equipment, such as PV
  inverters at night. It does *not* include appliances on standby.

The *ratio* sensors show the same split as a percentage of all power entering
the home, plus the share that is exported.

</SensorCard>

<SensorCard title="Debug readings" diagnostic sensors={[
  {name: 'Available power', unit: 'W', on: 'Debug sensors'},
  {name: 'Metering imbalance', unit: 'W', on: 'Debug sensors'},
]}>

**Available power** is all power entering the home: grid import + PV
production + battery discharge.

**Metering imbalance** is how much more your metered devices drew than your
sources supplied. Meters that update at slightly different moments make it
briefly non-zero, and Power Insight evens that out on its own. If it stays
large, a meter is probably set up wrong (an inverted sign, or a consumer
meter inside another metered circuit), and a repair issue tells you so.

</SensorCard>

## Cost

Every money sensor comes in a **Standard** and a **Levelized** flavour. See
[How it works](../how-it-works.md) for the difference. Sensors ending in
*rate* are money per hour right now; *Total* sensors add it up across
restarts.

What one kWh costs you right now, averaged over your whole energy mix. With
**Standard**, grid power counts at the grid price and your own power is free.

lifetime cost per kWh instead.

<details>
<summary>How the combined levelized totals are worked out</summary>

The combined *levelized* totals are not counted on their own. Each one is the
sum of every device's own levelized total, plus whatever devices you have since
removed contributed. That way, editing a device's lifetime values corrects the
past too (see the [correction factor](../how-it-works.md#the-correction-factor)),
and removing a device never wipes out what it saved.

While a device's own total has no value yet, for example just after a
restart, the combined total shows as **unavailable** rather than a partial
sum, which would look like a false drop in your statistics. A device whose
total you turned off is left out.

</details>

## Home base load

Turn on **Home base load** under *Options → Combined sensors → Power* to get a
separate device for everything without a sensor of its own: your fridge,
lights, router and so on. Its power is your total home consumption minus the
consumers you have added.

<SensorCards>

<SensorCard title="Base load" sensors={[
  {name: 'Power', unit: 'W', on: 'Home base load'},
  {name: 'Avoided cost rate', unit: 'EUR/h', on: 'Home base load'},
]}>

**Power** is what the rest of the house draws right now. Its attributes show
where that power comes from (grid, solar, battery). **Avoided cost rate** is
what your own power saved on that part of the house, at the grid price.

</SensorCard>

</SensorCards>
