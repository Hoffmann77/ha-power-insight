# Grid connection

The grid connection is the **import/export meter** of your home and the price
baseline for every cost and savings calculation. **Exactly one grid connection
is required** per energy mix — if you have two grid connections, model them as
two separate config entries.

Add it with **Add device → Grid connection**.

## Settings

### Power sensor

> Sensor with the grid power in W, kW or MW. Positive = import, negative =
> export.

This is the only always-required field. It must report instantaneous power (not
energy). Power Insight normalises W / kW / MW automatically.

### Invert power sign

> Turn on if your sensor uses the opposite sign.

Use this when your meter reports **import as negative** (or export as positive).

### Electricity price

> Sensor or number with the current price per kWh you pay for grid power. Required for cost, savings and financial return sensors.

This can be a live/dynamic price sensor (for example from a dynamic-tariff
integration) or a static `input_number`. Because Power Insight uses the *current*
value at every calculation, dynamic tariffs are fully supported.

The sensor must report a **price per unit of energy** as its unit of
measurement. `EUR/kWh`, `€/kWh`, `ct/kWh`, `EUR/MWh` and `EUR/Wh` (or the same
in any other currency) are all converted to a price per kWh; a sensor in any
other unit — or with no unit at all — is refused, because taken at face value
a `ct/kWh` tariff would make every cost a hundred times too large. An
`input_number` needs its unit of measurement set accordingly.

Power Insight does not convert currencies. If the price names a currency other
than the one Home Assistant is set to, a repair issue says so.

### Grid CO₂ intensity

> Sensor with the grid's current CO₂ intensity in g/kWh (e.g. from
> [Electricity Maps](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/co2signal/)).
> Optional; reserved for CO₂ sensors.

:::note

CO₂ sensors are **not implemented yet**, so this field currently has no
effect. It is safe to leave empty.

:::

### Export limit

> Optional: the most your grid connection may export, in W (e.g. 800 for a balcony system, or your grid operator's limit). Leave empty for no limit. Only used to value the solar energy a battery stores: without the battery, energy above the limit would have been curtailed.

Your meters already obey the limit, so it never changes where power is shown
going. It only decides how much of the solar energy a battery stores could
have been exported instead: the battery pays the export compensation for that
part, and nothing for the rest — see
[What a battery pays for the solar energy it stores](../advanced/costs-and-savings.md#what-a-battery-pays-for-the-solar-energy-it-stores).
Can be changed later under **Reconfigure**.

### Already accumulated

> Optional, in two sections. **Energy (kWh):** what the whole home exported
> to the grid and the average grid tariff over that period — your PV
> systems' and batteries' savings are calculated with this tariff, unless a
> device has its own.
> **Amounts:** the whole home's total savings and export compensation from
> your app. Both are split between your PV systems and batteries. See
> [Carried-over history](../advanced/history.md).

## Sensors

Turn these on in the integration's **Options → Grid sensors**. Import and
export both happen at your grid connection, so this one device carries
**both sides of the meter**.

### Power flow

<SensorCards>

<SensorCard title="Import and export" sensors={[
  {name: 'Import power', unit: 'W', on: 'Power split (W)'},
  {name: 'Export power', unit: 'W', on: 'Power split (W)'},
]}>

How much power you are **buying** from the grid right now, and how much
surplus you are **selling** back. Only one of the two is above 0 at any
moment.

</SensorCard>

<SensorCard title="Where the import goes" sensors={[
  {name: 'Import to home consumption', unit: 'W', on: 'Power split (W)'},
  {name: 'Import to batteries', unit: 'W', on: 'Power split (W)'},
  {name: 'Import to system standby', unit: 'W', on: 'Power split (W)'},
  {name: 'Import to home consumption ratio', unit: '%', on: 'Power split (%)'},
  {name: 'Import to batteries ratio', unit: '%', on: 'Power split (%)'},
  {name: 'Import to system standby ratio', unit: '%', on: 'Power split (%)'},
]}>

Splits the grid import by what it is used for: your
[appliances](../sensors.mdx#where-power-goes), charging a battery, or the idle
draw of your energy equipment. The *ratio* sensors show the same split as a
percentage **of the import**.

The *batteries* sensors only exist when at least one battery is allowed to
charge from the grid.

</SensorCard>

<SensorCard title="The grid's share of the home" sensors={[
  {name: 'Share of home consumption', unit: '%', on: 'Share of home totals (%)'},
  {name: 'Share of battery charging', unit: '%', on: 'Share of home totals (%)'},
  {name: 'Share of system standby', unit: '%', on: 'Share of home totals (%)'},
]}>

How much of **everything your home consumes** (or charges into batteries, or
loses to standby) is currently covered by the grid. If this reads 30 %, the
other 70 % comes from your solar and batteries.

</SensorCard>

</SensorCards>

### Cost and earnings

<SensorCards>

<SensorCard title="Import cost" sensors={[
  {name: 'Import cost rate', unit: 'EUR/h', on: 'Cost method: Standard'},
  {name: 'Total import cost', unit: 'EUR', on: 'Total costs'},
]}>

What your grid import costs per hour right now (import power × your current
electricity price), and the running total. Needs the
[Electricity price](#electricity-price) setting.

</SensorCard>

<SensorCard title="Export compensation" sensors={[
  {name: 'Export compensation rate', unit: 'EUR/h', on: 'Export compensation rate'},
  {name: 'Total export compensation', unit: 'EUR', on: 'Total export compensation'},
]}>

What your home earns per hour by exporting, across all exporting PV systems
and batteries, and the running total. Each device's own share appears on that
device.

</SensorCard>

</SensorCards>

:::info[No levelized costs for the grid]

Levelized costs (LCOE/LCOS) spread the price of hardware **you own** over its
lifetime. Grid power has no such hardware, so the grid only offers the
**Standard** cost method.

:::
