## Why this case exists

A battery is the only device that changes which side of the diagram it is on.
Everything else has a fixed role — a string produces, a load consumes — and the
engine can lean on that. A battery is a sink on one snapshot and a source on
the next, and both have to work without special-casing it.

Charging is the easy half: it is a sink, and an unrestricted one, so it takes
the same raw mix as the metered load beside it. Discharging is where the
snapshot model runs into its own limits. The energy coming out of `bat1` was
paid for at some earlier time, at some earlier mix, and an engine that only
knows about *now* cannot see any of that. So it does the only defensible thing:
prices the discharge at the battery's flat levelized cost of storage, and
reports its **marginal** price as zero, because at this instant nothing is being
bought to produce it.

The idle snapshot is a smaller point but a real one. `bat1` reads exactly 0 W,
which makes it neither a source nor a sink. It appears in neither flow group
rather than being counted as a zero-watt member of both.
