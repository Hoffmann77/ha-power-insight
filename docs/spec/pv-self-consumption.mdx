## Why this case exists

This is the first case with a *choice* in it. Two sources are supplying one
sink, and the engine has to say in what proportion — the answer being the raw
availability of the sources, with no restriction anywhere to complicate it.
Every restricted case later in the ladder is a departure from exactly this, and
if the tiered logic in the solver is working, this is what it must collapse to
when there is nothing to tier.

Two sources are also the minimum for the money layer to say anything
interesting. With the grid alone, what power costs now and what it costs
levelized are the same number. Add a PV system and they come apart: `coe` falls as
the free fuel displaces the tariff, while `lcoe` carries the PV system's capital
cost whether or not the sun is out.

The standby snapshot is the sharpest thing here. A PV system drawing 20 W is not
producing −20 W — it is a **sink**, and it is served from the grid like any
other. Which means the saving rate goes *negative*: at that instant the solar
installation is costing its owner money.
