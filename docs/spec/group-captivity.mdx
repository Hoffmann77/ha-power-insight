## Why this case exists

This is the case the max-flow solver exists for, and it is worth walking
through slowly.

`bat_a` and `bat_b` may each charge from either string. Neither is individually
stuck — either string could cover either battery. But together they draw exactly
what the two strings make, so as a *pair* they need every local watt. Any
allocation that gives local power to somebody else leaves one of them short.

Asking "what must **this** sink take from this source?" one sink at a time
cannot see that. It is Hall's condition, which quantifies over every *subset* of
sinks, and no per-sink patch reproduces it. Watch the home base load in the
first snapshot: it is unrestricted and could physically take solar, but it is
served entirely from the grid because the pair have first claim.

The second snapshot then overloads the same wiring deliberately, and that is
where the model runs out of settled answers.
