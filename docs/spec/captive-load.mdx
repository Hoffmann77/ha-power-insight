## Why this case exists

Three adapters is the smallest wiring in which a restriction can be honoured,
and — one reading later — the smallest in which one can fail. Both belong here
rather than further up the ladder, because with three devices you can follow
every watt of the deficit by eye.

When the PV system covers the captive load, the rule is visible in one line:
`cons1` runs on solar alone, and the *unrestricted* base load is pushed onto the
grid to make room. A naive implementation that shared each source in proportion
would hand `cons1` a slice of grid power it is not permitted to touch, and give
the base load solar it has no priority claim to. The two implementations
separate immediately.

The second snapshot asks what happens when the restriction is simply not true.
`cons1` draws 500 W and its only permitted source makes 300 W. The engine does
not refuse to answer and does not silently pretend the extra 200 W was solar —
it attributes the draw as the meters actually show it and reports the 200 W as
a **restriction deficit**. That figure is not an engine failure. It is the
engine saying the meters disagree with how you told it your energy manager
behaves.
