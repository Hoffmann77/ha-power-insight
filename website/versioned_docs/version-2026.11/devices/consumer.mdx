# Consumer

A consumer represents an electrical load — an appliance, EV charger, heat pump,
etc. Power Insight tracks how much power it draws and where that power comes from.

Add it with **Add device → Consumer**.

:::warning[Under development]

Consumer support is still being built out, and more consumer sensors are
planned.

:::

## Settings

### Name

> For example "Rooftop" or "Heat pump". Used in the device and sensor names.

### Power sensor

> Sensor that reports this device's power in W, kW or MW. Negative = power drawn
> by the consumer. Most smart plugs report their draw as a positive value — turn
> on **Invert power sign** for those.

### Invert power sign

> Turn on if your sensor uses the opposite sign.

### Power sources / Powered by

> **Whole mix** — the power comes from all sources in proportion to what they supply.
>
> **Specific devices** — only from the sources you select below, e.g. a battery that charges from solar only.

> The sources that power this consumer. Only used with **Specific devices**.

Default: **Whole mix**. Choose **Specific devices** for a load your energy
manager runs on particular sources — for example a smart plug that only switches
on with excess solar. The grid and your PV systems are selectable.

The selection describes what you expect your energy manager to do, not a hard
limit. If the consumer draws more than its selected sources supply — say it is
set to solar only but keeps running after the sun goes down — that power is
still counted, as coming from your other sources, and the watts show up in
the `restriction_deficit` attribute of its operating-cost sensors. See
[When a device draws from outside its sources](../advanced/power-attribution.md#when-a-device-draws-from-outside-its-sources).

:::note[Reconfigure prompt]

When you remove a grid or PV device this consumer is restricted to, Power
Insight raises a repair issue asking you to **reconfigure** the consumer.

:::

A consumer has no cost, price, or lifetime fields of its own — its cost is
derived from the mix of sources currently supplying it (grid / solar / battery).

## Sensors

Turn these on in the integration's **Options → Consumer sensors**. The choice
applies to every consumer.

### Where its power comes from

<SensorCards>

<SensorCard title="Source mix right now" sensors={[
  {name: 'Power share from <source>', unit: '%', on: 'Power sources (%)'},
  {name: 'Power from <source>', unit: 'W', on: 'Power from each source (W)'},
]}>

One sensor per source in your home — the grid, each PV system and each
battery — named after it, e.g. *Power share from Rooftop*. They answer "what
is the heat pump running on right now?": say 55 % solar and 45 % grid, or the
same split in watts.

The watts add up to the consumer's draw. While your meters disagree they can
come out slightly below its own meter — see *Metering imbalance* on the
[whole home](home.mdx).

</SensorCard>

<SensorCard title="Source mix over time" sensors={[
  {name: 'Energy from <source>', unit: 'kWh', on: 'Energy from each source (kWh)'},
  {name: 'Energy share from <source>', unit: '%', on: 'Energy sources (%)'},
  {name: 'Energy from removed devices', unit: 'kWh', on: 'Energy from each source (kWh)'},
  {name: 'Energy share from removed devices', unit: '%', on: 'Energy sources (%)'},
]}>

Running totals of how much energy came from each source — "312 kWh of solar
and 540 kWh from the grid" — and the lifetime mix that makes (37 % solar,
63 % grid). For a daily or monthly mix, put the *Energy from …* sensors
behind a [utility meter](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/utility_meter/).

The *removed devices* sensors only appear after you remove a source; they
keep the energy it supplied so the totals don't shrink.

</SensorCard>

<SensorCard title="Consumption share" sensors={[
  {name: 'Consumption share', unit: '%', on: 'Consumption share (%)'},
]}>

This consumer's slice of everything your home consumes right now.

</SensorCard>

</SensorCards>

:::warning[Don't add *Energy from …* to the Energy dashboard]

These sensors split energy the consumer's own meter already counts. Adding
them next to that meter counts the same energy twice.

:::

### Cost and savings

<SensorCards>

<SensorCard title="Operating cost" sensors={[
  {name: 'Operating cost rate', unit: 'EUR/h', on: 'Cost method: Standard'},
  {name: 'Levelized operating cost rate', unit: 'EUR/h', on: 'Cost method: Levelized'},
  {name: 'Total operating cost', unit: 'EUR', on: 'Total costs'},
  {name: 'Total levelized operating cost', unit: 'EUR', on: 'Total costs + Levelized'},
]}>

What running this consumer costs, based on where its power comes from. With
**Standard**, grid power costs the live grid price and your own solar and
battery power is free. With **Levelized**, your own power is priced at the
lifetime cost per kWh of the device that supplied it.

</SensorCard>

<SensorCard title="Avoided cost" sensors={[
  {name: 'Avoided cost rate', unit: 'EUR/h', on: 'Savings method: Standard'},
  {name: 'Total avoided cost', unit: 'EUR', on: 'Total savings'},
  {name: 'Avoided cost from <source>', unit: 'EUR/h', on: 'Avoided cost by source'},
  {name: 'Total avoided cost from <source>', unit: 'EUR', on: 'Avoided cost by source + Total savings'},
  {name: 'Total avoided cost from removed devices', unit: 'EUR', on: 'Avoided cost by source + Total savings'},
]}>

What this consumer did **not** pay the grid because your solar or battery
powered it. The *from …* sensors split that by PV system and battery (the
grid has none: it is what was avoided).

Your PV systems and batteries report the same money as their own savings, so
don't add the two together.

</SensorCard>

</SensorCards>

:::note[`restriction_deficit` attribute]

With **Power sources** set to *Specific devices*, both operating-cost rate
sensors carry a `restriction_deficit` attribute: the watts the consumer is
drawing from outside its selected sources. See
[When a device draws from outside its sources](../advanced/power-attribution.md#when-a-device-draws-from-outside-its-sources).

:::

<details>
<summary>What happens when you remove a source</summary>

The consumer's *Energy from …* sensor for that source is disabled, keeping its
history; delete it yourself if you no longer want it. Its final value moves
into *Energy from removed devices*, so the consumer's totals don't lose it.

*Energy from removed devices* has no state class: it steps up once at each
removal, and that energy is already in the old sensor's history, so long-term
statistics would count it twice. A total that was not counting when its
source was removed (option off, or entity disabled) has nothing to keep.

</details>
