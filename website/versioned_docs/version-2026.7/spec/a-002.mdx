## Why this case exists

One restriction, two opposite consequences — which is why both snapshots share a
wiring.

When the captive battery's allowed source is producing, the battery **depletes
it first**: it takes all of `pv1`, and the unrestricted consumer is left with
the grid. A naive implementation that shared each source proportionally would
hand the battery a slice of grid power it is not allowed to touch, so this
snapshot separates the two implementations immediately.

When that same source drops into standby it stops being a source at all. The
battery now has nowhere legal to draw from, and rather than dividing by zero the
row collapses to zeros and the shortfall is reported as a **restriction
deficit**.
